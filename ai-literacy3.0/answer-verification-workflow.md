# Answer Verification Workflow

“Verify This Answer” appears for applicable factual AI answers and opens the private “Check Before You Trust” workspace. Students preserve the original, review atomic claims, classify claim/evidence types, find and open sources, review authority/originality/relevance/currentness, compare independent sources, record disagreement, assign claim statuses, revise, and save a versioned record.

The six lesson phases are Learn, Observe, Practise, Explain, Apply, and Reflect. This static prototype uses synthetic fixtures and in-memory records; it has no live search/provider, durable storage, or truth certification.

Step 15 opens from a reviewed claim and source set, then returns student-confirmed weight/claim/revision updates to the versioned Step 14 record.

## Step 16 — Claim–Evidence Map

Step 16 reuses Step 14 claims and returns only student-confirmed, versioned updates; historical verification states are never overwritten.

## Step 17 — AI Citation and Disclosure

Step 17 verified-source links reference Step 14 records; an AI-generated reference remains blocked until existence and claim support are verified.

## Step 18 — AI Brainstorming

Step 18 assumptions remain explicitly unverified and may open Step 14 rather than being presented as established facts.

## Step 19 — AI Feedback

Step 19 unsupported-claim issues may open Step 14; feedback never invents or automatically verifies a source.

## Step 20 — Revision With Judgment

Step 20 new or strengthened claims open Step 14 and remain pending rather than becoming verified automatically.

## Community projects

Project sources and factual outputs reuse verification records. Failed, outdated, unsupported, or invented-source results feed the test and revision matrix.
