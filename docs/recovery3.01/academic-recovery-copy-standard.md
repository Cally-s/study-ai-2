# Academic Recovery Copy Standard

Step 17 promises focus, not full-course coverage, question prediction or score improvement. “Topics Not Covered Tonight” and stopping on time use neutral language.

Step 16 says the student may stop after ten minutes or early. Elapsed, partial, blocked and no-progress states use neutral language and never imply failure or insufficient effort.

Step 15 requires specific verbs and observable completion. “Blocked” means information, material, prerequisite or help is needed—not failure. Vague, blaming and time-spent-as-completion language is prohibited.

## Central design message

“The plan can change. You have not failed. Let’s choose the next achievable action.”

## Approved supportive wording

Use: “You do not have to solve everything at once,” “Some work may need to wait,” “Asking for help is progress,” “You remain in control of what is shared,” and “A realistic plan protects sleep and essential responsibilities.”

## Prohibited shame-based wording

Never call a student lazy, weak, careless, irresponsible, a failure or unlikely to succeed. Do not use guilt, comparison, broken-streak language, “no excuses,” or instructions to fix everything overnight. Do not infer anxiety, depression, attention difficulty or burnout.

## Notification wording

Approved: “A due date has passed. You can review your options and choose the next achievable action.” The notification must not expose Recovery Mode on a lock screen, change assignment state or contact staff.

## Error wording

Approved: “StudySpark could not start the recovery plan right now. Your assignments and current work were not changed.” Offer Try Again, Save My Choice on This Device, Return, or Continue Existing Recovery Plan when those actions are truthful.

## No-grade-guarantee wording

Recovery planning does not promise a particular mark, improvement, completion result or academic outcome.

## No-deadline-guarantee wording

“StudySpark cannot guarantee that late work will be accepted or that a deadline will be changed.”

Step 2 asks what kind of catch-up help is needed without asking why the student procrastinated or requiring absence, health, family or diagnostic details. “I Am Overwhelmed” changes presentation load only and is not diagnostic or emergency language.

Step 3 canonical copy includes “We can begin with incomplete information,” “You do not need to list everything at once,” “Incomplete information is enough to begin,” and “This is enough to begin.”
## Step 4 work-inventory copy

Use “Let’s Gather the Work That May Matter,” explicitly permit incomplete lists and make `Status Unknown` easy to select. Use “Student-reported status,” never “unreliable status.” Past-due uncertainty uses text rather than colour alone. When substantial overdue work has unknown late acceptance, use exactly: “We should confirm this with your teacher before spending several hours on it.” Never imply the question was sent.
## Step 6 capacity copy

Ask for actual time, validate no-time/unsure and explain envelope versus buffer. Always say “This is a planning limit, not a target you must reach.” When workload exceeds capacity say it does not fit, followed by “That does not mean you failed.” Never praise larger totals or suggest sacrificing essentials.

## Step 7 feasibility copy

Use the canonical compassionate gap message and distinguish Manageable, Tight, Not currently possible, and Needs more information. Never guarantee completion, grades, extensions, acceptance, or describe a capacity gap as personal failure.

Step 8 says “We should confirm this with your teacher before spending several hours on it” for substantial uncertain old work. Confirmed, Confirmed by You, Estimated, Assumption, Unknown, Conflicting Information and Needs Recheck remain explicit and supportive.
# Step 9 copy

Use “Choose What Matters First,” the five category labels, visible reasons/factors/unknowns, and “what could change” language. Consider Releasing must always say private plan only and never imply deletion, abandonment, failure, or official status change.

Step 10 uses “Why This Priority?”, “Why This Category,” “What Could Change This Recommendation?”, “Why Isn’t This in Another Category?”, “Change Priority,” and “Restore StudySpark Recommendation.” Wording stays neutral, concise, source-supported and never exposes private model reasoning.

Prerequisite copy describes a possible gap for the current task. It must not blame the student or imply laziness, irresponsibility, low intelligence, disability, permanent weakness, or failure.

Repair copy says “ready for the current task,” never “mastered.” Needs More Review is supportive, not failure. Skips, I Do Not Know and requests for human help are neutral choices.

Minimum completion is success; Stretch is always explicitly optional. Partial or blocked work says the plan can change, never that the student failed, broke a streak or lacked responsibility.

Day themes are descriptive, exact dates are explicit and no-work/catch-up/buffer copy avoids guilt or calendar-fullness pressure.
## Missed-week copy

Use “You do not need to recreate every missed class activity” and “The goal is to reconnect you with current lessons—not to reproduce every minute you missed.” Avoid blame, attendance-compliance language, backlog walls and promises that everything can be recovered in seven days.
## Missed-month copy

Lead with the required safe-seven-day limitation and current-access principle. Describe clarification, preparation and waiting positively; never predict pass/fail/grades, promise recovery, blame the gap or imply support approval.
## Prerequisite-help copy

Say “smallest concept blocking your current work,” never easy/weak/failed or fixed learning-style labels. Describe unsuccessful explanations neutrally and human help as responsive live support.
## Overwhelmed copy

Use short, one-idea sentences and the required calm messages. Never say calm down, overreacting, failed, should have started, must finish or use countdown/guilt pressure.
## Teacher-message copy

Use concise neutral respectful language without excessive apology, self-blame, coercion, promises or deceptive excuses. Clearly label student-reported/proposed/unknown facts.
# Step 23 copy

Parent summaries use neutral, strengths-based, source-grounded wording. They avoid motivation, effort, character, blame, comparison, guilt, punishment, promises, and invented progress. Copy, Download, Print, Link Created, Shared, Delivered, Viewed, and Acknowledged are distinct states.
# Step 24 check-in copy

Required wording recognizes partial progress, explains that Blocked is not failure, removes long-reflection pressure, and says the plan can change. Avoid failure, effort, productivity, streak, guilt, punishment, all-nighter, and “make up for it” framing.
# Step 25 recalculation copy

Use “The plan changed,” “progress is still saved,” “needs more time,” “lower-priority work moved,” “deadline may be at risk,” and “finish for today.” Never use failure, further-behind, lost-streak, make-up, noncompliance, effort, guilt, or punishment framing.
# Step 26 blocked copy

Use “choose one action that can unlock it,” “Blocked does not mean failed,” another-ready-task support, no-indefinite-plan wording, and neutral unresolved-action copy. Never blame, diagnose, infer effort/ability, threaten, or demand private explanations.

## Step 27 — Right-Time Human Support

This flow may create one explained primary recommendation and up to two alternatives when authority, context, live interaction, formal support, safety, or supervised work is needed. Students may ask at any time. Only valid, accessible, language-appropriate, materially distinct attempts count toward the two-attempt recommendation and three-attempt AI stop. All sharing is previewed and student controlled; no message, booking, parent notification, official decision, attendance, service hour, Help Credit, or plan change occurs automatically.

## Step 28 — Private Recovery Progress Recognition

Only specific, meaningful, source-versioned actions with sufficient evidence may receive brief recognition explaining why the action matters. Partial, prepared, uploaded, booked, proposed, or provisional states are never overstated as completed, sent, submitted, attended, accepted, approved, or active. Recognition is private, cumulative, non-resetting, dismissible, preference controlled, bundled at natural stopping points, and suppressed during focused work, assessments, meetings, tutoring, sensitive forms, and immediate-safety support. It creates no streak, points, rank, badge, public sharing, grade, matching effect, service hour, Help Credit, safety decision, or automatic notification.

## Step 29 — Better Recovery Metrics

This integration contributes only authoritative, versioned, deduplicated events to clearly defined metric cards. Draft, proposed, uploaded, booked, identified, timed, partial, or provisional states are never overstated as sent, approved, submitted, attended, addressed, resolved, repaired, or completed. Historical progress remains visible beside reopened/current state. Cards may overlap but are never summed; there is no global score, unstable backlog percentage, streak, points, rank, comparison, automatic sharing, or consequential use.

## Step 30 — Supportive Notifications

Academic Recovery reminders are optional, explicitly enabled per channel/category, generic on lock screens by default, capped, deduplicated, expiring, and revalidated against current source versions. All categories respect quiet hours and protected stopping time; only student-selected confirmed session exceptions apply. Focus, assessment, safety, stale/blocked/material/authority states suppress delivery. Disable All, Snooze, Dismiss, and notification opening carry no penalty and cannot change tasks, plans, messages, bookings, submissions, attendance, metrics, recognition, grades, matching, service hours, Help Credits, fraud, integrity, or safety decisions. Nothing is automatically shared with another person.
