# Who Is Doing the Thinking?

## Purpose and learning goal
This 25–35 minute Understand lesson teaches students to distinguish AI assistance from answer replacement by identifying student contribution, AI contribution, verification, disclosure, assignment policy, and human responsibility. Human Agency (`competency-1`) is primary; Human Accountability, Safe and Responsible Use, Application Skills, and AI Foundations are secondary.

## Six phases
Learn introduces meaningful agency. Observe compares Scenario A (AI brainstorms science-fair topics; the student chooses, researches, and designs) with Scenario B (AI writes the complete report; the student submits it unchanged). Practise maps six workflows. Explain requires the student’s comparison. Apply creates a private AI-Assistance Plan. Reflect considers future choices without forced personal disclosure.

## Known limitations
This static prototype uses in-memory records and deterministic feedback. It has no production authentication, database, policy service, speech provider, localization service, service worker, or competency ingestion. Formal integrity and competency decisions remain human/server responsibilities.
# Verification connection

“Why AI Can Invent Information” gives students a structured way to carry out the verification responsibility introduced in this Human Agency lesson.
# Bias-review connection

Step 8 applies Human Agency to deciding whether AI belongs in a task and where human review, correction, appeal, and final responsibility must remain.
# Privacy agency

Step 9 keeps the student in control of redaction, final preview, cancel, secure-channel choice, and intentional submission.
# Step 10 extension

AI Assistance Versus Original Work applies Human Agency to specific assessed skills, policy, disclosure, readiness, and workflow redesign across assignment types.
# Policy awareness extension

Step 11 strengthens Human Agency by requiring verified task-specific permission before AI creates submission material.
