# Recovery Feasibility Analysis

Test rescue feasibility reports topics not covered instead of compressing the course. A ten-minute start or partial review never makes remaining preparation appear feasible.

After decomposition, feasibility uses remaining action upper estimates and preserves protected buffer. Small actions cannot make the underlying workload appear smaller.

## Purpose

Step 7 measures whether current remaining work fits confirmed focused recovery time. It does not prioritize every item or generate a schedule.

## Formula and data

`Recovery Load = estimated remaining work minutes / confirmed focused recovery minutes`. Integer minutes and a 10,000-unit rational scale avoid binary floating-point authority. Confirmed and provisional work remain separate; accepted, no-longer-required, archived, and submitted-with-no-action work is excluded. Unknown estimates remain null and prevent a false Manageable result.

The denominator is Step 6 focused-task capacity, after the planning envelope, explicit breaks, setup/transition, uncertainty, commitments, and protected buffer. Raw availability and calendar gaps are not used.

## Classification and bottlenecks

The versioned default policy uses Manageable through 0.85 and Tight through 1.00. Minimum work above capacity, zero capacity with work, or a confirmed deadline bottleneck is Not currently possible without changes. Ranges and material uncertainty produce provisional results. Each known deadline compares cumulative required work, including unique prerequisites, with confirmed pre-deadline capacity.

## Teacher decisions and recalculation

Decision flags identify unknown status, acceptance, deadline, estimate, instructions, materials, responses, prerequisites, and bottlenecks. Questions remain drafts. Inventory, capacity, date, dependency, policy, timezone, or source-version changes make the result OUTDATED.

## Known limitations

The current executable browser contract has no production server, database, trusted clock/DST disambiguation, authorized course source validation, durable sync, rate limiter, or human accessibility evidence.

Step 8 material fact changes invalidate this analysis. Old Recovery Load results cannot authorize prioritization; production recalculation must consume current inventory/capacity/verification versions.
# Step 9 integration

Transparent prioritization consumes the current analysis ID, capacity snapshot version, deadline feasibility, classification, focused recovery minutes, and protected buffer. An Outdated feasibility analysis cannot authorize a new priority review. Do Now overflow never consumes the protected buffer or becomes a schedule.

Step 10 capacity claims cite the feasibility and capacity versions and disclose overflow without private commitment details. A changed analysis invalidates the explanation.

Concept bridges add five to twenty minutes of explicit work, preserve required breaks, never consume protected buffer, and invalidate feasibility whenever their duration or status changes.

Step 12 repair minutes enter workload once. Completed minutes remain historical and remaining repair minutes become zero; shared links do not multiply the repair duration.

Step 13 cannot hide infeasible urgent work in Stretch. It displays unfitted work separately and returns to scope, teacher decisions or capacity rather than compressing estimates.

Step 14 validates the upper action range on each date; days that do not fit cannot become Ready and are replanned without adding an eighth day silently.
