# Transparent Recovery Prioritization

Ten-minute selection respects reviewed action order; shortness, grade weight and overdue state are never sole selection reasons.

An accepted decomposition may change the remaining estimate. Priority and its explanation are revalidated when needed; categories do not change before student review.

## Purpose

Step 9 turns current work, capacity, feasibility, and verification records into a private, reproducible review. It does not create a final task breakdown or calendar.

## Five categories

The fixed display order is **Do Now**, **Ask First**, **Do Next**, **Schedule Later**, and **Consider Releasing**. Completed, accepted, inactive, and officially no-longer-required work is shown separately. Decision precedence is exclusion, Ask First, Consider Releasing, Do Now, Do Next, then Schedule Later.

## Factor model and ordering

Every card exposes deadline proximity, acceptance/requirement, grade impact, dependencies, actionability, blocking impact, teacher clarification, student importance, completion leverage, remaining time, deadline fit, verification readiness, materials, instructions, capacity, status, and optional/replaced evidence. Category-specific comparison uses deadline band, student importance, remaining time, then title. There is no stored or displayed aggregate score.

## Student control

Students can set private importance, request a category override, and reorder with Move up/Move down buttons. The system recommendation stays visible. Substantial provisional work cannot be overridden into confirmed Do Now.

## Known limitations

The prototype is memory-backed. It has no authenticated server, database, school source refresh, durable offline sync, organization scope, production rate limiter, or human accessibility evidence. Estimates remain estimates. Final decomposition and scheduling belong to Step 10.

## Step 10 explanation layer

Every active category and rank now has a versioned claim-based explanation. Step 10 reads this priority decision and never recomputes it. Student changes require a private impact preview and confirmation through the existing override system.

## Step 11 prerequisite effect

Barrier evidence never silently changes priority. Creating or changing a concept bridge invalidates the current feasibility and priority outputs so the student must review recalculated recommendations.

Repair may be an immediate action while its task remains Ask First. A successful repair still goes through the priority engine; it never forces Do Now.

Step 13 consumes priority decisions but does not equate every Do Now task with Minimum membership. Capacity, verification, dependencies and the smallest stabilization set remain additional hard constraints.

Step 14 assigns reviewed actions to days; a theme or empty calendar gap never overrides the priority engine.
## Missed-month order

Visible lexicographic precedence protects safety/capacity, resolves decisions, restores current access, repairs the smallest prerequisite, then handles current work, assessments, accepted/replacement work and backlog. No opaque viability or recovery score is used.
## Teacher priority request

A message may list one to three selected same-course items and ask which one or two matter first. It never exports the full priority list or treats a proposed answer as official.
# Step 25 recalculation integration

Revised priorities reuse existing categories/reasons without hidden scores. They preserve current-unit/test/prerequisite/current required work, remove Stretch/lower pressure first, keep urgent unfitted work visible, and require student review.
