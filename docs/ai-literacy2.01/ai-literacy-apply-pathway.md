# Apply Pathway

Students practise accountable use, privacy and policy checks, source verification, and mapping inputs, review points, and failure modes. The four competencies are Human accountability, Safe and responsible use, Application skills, and Architecture design.

Starter: **Verify an AI Answer**. The learner separates fact, assumption, interpretation, and example; checks claim-to-source support; revises; explains; and reflects. When no source was used, the interface states: “No external source was used for this explanation.” Sources are never fabricated.

Diagnostic evidence about unsupported claims, verification, sources, privacy, assistance, and disclosure may produce positive Apply practice suggestions. Suggestions are not levels.

Verified claims, source selection, accountable integrity decisions, and documented revisions can become student-reviewed evidence.

“Why AI Invents Information” is the initial structured Apply lesson and uses an explicitly synthetic correction example.
# Step 12: Prompt With Purpose

Prompt With Purpose applies the prior privacy, ownership, verification, and policy lessons through a Quick Builder, Detailed Visual Builder, private sandbox review, version comparison, and evidence-candidate workflow.

# Step 13: Revise, Compare, Verify

Students extend Step 12 with revision hypotheses, immutable prompt/response history, semantic comparisons, variability warnings, verification, tradeoff analysis, and stopping decisions.

# Step 14: Check Before You Trust

Students decompose answers into atomic claims, review suitable evidence and independent sources, preserve disagreement/unresolved states, revise deliberately, and save versioned records.

# Step 15

Students compare what each source contributes, its limitations/dependence, and its claim-specific weight without universal rankings.

## Step 16 — Claim–Evidence Map

Step 16 maps Claim → Evidence → Source → Support Reason → Limitation and preserves student-controlled revisions without scores.

## Step 17 — AI Citation and Disclosure

Step 17 separates factual citation, AI-output attribution, and AI-use disclosure, then builds a student-reviewed receipt without automatic sharing.

## Step 18 — AI Brainstorming

Step 18 uses bounded idea seeds followed by explained selection, rejection, student contribution, provenance, and independent development.

## Step 19 — AI Feedback

Step 19 begins with student work, links feedback to evidence, and requires student decisions, edits, explanations, version comparison, and voice review.

## Step 20 — Revision With Judgment

Step 20 evaluates used, partial, rejected, and alternative feedback decisions through semantic comparison, provenance, voice, and verification.
