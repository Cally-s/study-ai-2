# Teacher AI Literacy Dashboard

## Step 60 badge review
The dashboard may show authorized review requests and criterion gaps without rank, urgency-by-speed, badge counts, candidate comparisons, or popularity. Candidate readiness is not an award and creates no grade/discipline action.

## Step 59 visibility
Teachers see only intentionally submitted portfolio snapshots and items they are separately authorized to review. Public plans, private drafts, engagement metrics, and unrelated items stay hidden; no portfolio ranking or completeness inference is produced.

## Step 58 performance summaries
The dashboard shows criterion-level pathway evidence, first clarifications, feedback/revision state, review/recognition state, and submission version after privacy thresholds. Counts, time, prompt/source/receipt volume, prototype size, risk scores, opaque global scores, and rankings are excluded.

The dashboard supports evidence-based instructional planning through exactly nine areas: Assigned AI Literacy Modules; Competencies Demonstrated; Scenario-Assessment Results; Source-Verification Activities; Student Reflection Submissions; Capstone Progress; Assignment AI Policies; Submitted AI Use Receipts; and Areas Requiring Instruction. It never calculates cheating probability or ranks AI use. Teacher notice: “This dashboard shows submitted learning evidence and instructional patterns. It does not calculate cheating probability, rank students by AI use, or provide automatic proof of mastery or misconduct.”

## Step 55 — Teacher Review Instead of Automatic Discipline

Trusted structured policy mismatches extend Step 40 with an educational reminder, server-permitted alternatives, optional separately stored student context, explicit published-rule escalation, minimal review packages, authorized human outcomes, and neutral versioned resolutions. Simple reminders and changed requests do not escalate by default. StudySpark never infers intent or misconduct, sends full conversations/private artifacts, uses behavioral signals or frequency scores, applies consequences, changes grades, restricts accounts, notifies parents, or creates discipline. Accessibility and safety remain separate.

## Step 56 — Teacher Lesson Tools
The dashboard links the `Teacher Lesson Tools` workspace for curriculum design and human review. Its drafts remain private; publishing, assignment, source approval, policy use, and assessment are separate teacher actions. Dashboard evidence is reused without activity, time, risk, probability, ranking, or usage proxies.
# Step 63 dashboard boundary

The dashboard shows activity-level accessibility coverage and unresolved publication issues, not student diagnoses, private profiles, language/reading ability, playback positions, raw transcripts/audio, accessibility usage, or rankings. Accessibility reports are product-quality/workflow signals and never performance, engagement, integrity, or discipline signals.
# Step 64 dashboard privacy

Teachers may see aggregate package availability, validation failures, and acknowledged synchronization/human-review needs. They do not see network speed/history, device/location/income inference, storage choices, unsynchronized drafts, local paths, raw content/audio, or connectivity scores. Offline delay is never participation, engagement, integrity, or risk evidence.
## Step 70 success measures

Authorized course and assignment views may show privacy-suppressed Responsible AI Learning Success Measures with exact numerator, denominator, window, definition version, completeness, and limitations. They exclude private prompts, conversations, receipt drafts, exact privacy findings, accessibility diagnoses, usage-success cards, and rankings. Claim confusion and independent-transfer patterns support instruction, not discipline.
