# Language Bridge

Step 22 requires official English privacy and control terms to remain visible for important notices. Supplemental translations must be labelled and must preserve purpose, recipients, retention, decline, deletion limits, contact, and policy/safety qualifications.

Status: PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED. Automated fixture tests pass; manual verification is PENDING.

Language Bridge is optional and uses a neutral **Home or bridge language** selection. It never infers home language, nationality, ethnicity, citizenship, immigration status, religion, English proficiency, ability, disability or financial status. StudySpark remains usable in English without configuration.

Six controlled modes exist: English only, Home language only, English first then home language, Home language first then English, Side-by-side bilingual, and English with translated keywords. English is the academic terminology language for this phase. Original English and required English academic terms remain visible through the English block and/or Important Academic Terms glossary.

Every artifact references the original source version/hash, Step 10 contract/hash, Step 11 explanation level, mode, bridge language, policy and terminology versions. Both language blocks use one level and one semantic source block. A level is generated from the original, never a translation.

The static prototype implements English-only generally and a reviewed development-only Simplified Chinese photosynthesis fixture plus an approved four-term glossary. It does not invent general translations. French, Spanish, Arabic and Traditional Chinese appear as glossary-only/architecture entries. Unsupported full translation retains the original with an honest error.

AI-style output carries the required error notice and is never called certified or teacher reviewed. Active assessments default to blocked unless explicitly permitted. Official/high-stakes English remains authoritative and an AI-only translation is refused. The original, comparison, glossary, per-language Listen controls, confirmed default save and private local problem notice are available.

Side-by-side content stacks on narrow/high-density layouts, uses language/direction attributes, logical CSS, LTR isolation for formulas/code and forced-colour borders. Real screen-reader, RTL content, 200% zoom, TTS voices, offline/cache, low-bandwidth and browser journeys remain PENDING.
# Step 13 vocabulary cards

Cards use the selected bridge language only for a sense-specific approved translation and never replace English. A translated term can map back to its English sense only where registry data exists. General cross-language reverse lookup remains unavailable.
# Step 14 canonical response mapping

The six Language Bridge modes map one-to-one to the multilingual response enum. Step 14 delegates generation/validation to this original-grounded service rather than adding another translator. Unsupported full languages retain English and approved keyword/glossary alternatives.
# Step 15 bandwidth behavior

Language Bridge generates only requested mode/language and no audio/images. Current validated in-memory fixtures may remain during an open page; new general translation/offline cache is unavailable. English terms are never removed to reduce payload.
Step 17 bilingual packages record requested languages, retain English academic terminology and use `lang`/`dir`; only requested variants are generated.
Step 18 generates only the requested language, retains English academic terminology in vocabulary metadata and uses `lang`/`dir`; no automatic translation is performed.
Step 19 bilingual schemas retain English academic terms and show the translation notice. Actual translated audio requires validated text and a matching licensed voice.
Step 20 multilingual guides require validated canonical translations; unsupported languages retain English text and are not generated automatically.
Step 21 Translate/Show Bilingual use target metadata from the original and preserve English academic terms; a validated provider result is required.
