QUICK START GUIDE

How StudySpark works

Everything you need to go from notes to a confident test day.

01

Create your account

Enter your name, email, grade, and main subjects. Your information is stored only in this browser.

02

Add your notes

Open My Notes, choose a subject, and paste or type your class material. Save or analyze it for key ideas.

03

Generate flashcards

Choose a subject and generate cards. Flip each card, then mark it "Know it" or "Need review."

04

Take a quiz

Create a five-question quiz from a subject. Submit your answers to see a score and clear explanations.

05

Build a study plan

Add your test date, topics, and available minutes. StudySpark creates a daily sequence of focused tasks.

06

Track your progress

Every saved note, reviewed card, and quiz score updates your progress dashboard automatically.

07

How to Use Folders

Open the Folders page from the sidebar, then click Create Folder. Name the folder, choose a color, and optionally choose a parent folder. Open a folder to view everything inside it. Use the three-dot menu to rename, move, recolor, or delete it. You can also create folders using the + button in the sidebar.

08

How to Update Your Courses

Open Account Settings, select or remove courses, then click Save courses. To create your own class, click "+ Add Custom Course," enter a unique name up to 30 characters, and click Add. Custom courses appear immediately across notes, files, flashcards, quizzes, plans, and the dashboard. You can rename or delete custom courses later.

09

How to Use Guest Mode

Click Continue as Guest to start without an account. No account is required, and your work saves only on this browser and device. Guest data may be lost if browser data is cleared. Create an account later if you want more permanent saving, and you can move guest work into that account later.

10

How to Upload Study Files

Open Notes or Quizzes, choose Upload File, then select or drag in a PDF, Word document, PowerPoint, text file, or image. Choose a course and folder before saving or generating a quiz. Uploaded files can be renamed, moved, opened, deleted, analyzed, or used to generate flashcards and quizzes. File contents are stored in this browser on this device.

11

How Your Learning Profile Works

Your profile starts blank and improves as you take quizzes and use study tools. You can add study preferences manually, while strong and weak topics are calculated from performance after enough questions. StudySpark uses the profile to personalize study plans and AI Coach advice.

12

How StudySpark Learns From Your Study Habits

The profile begins as Unknown. StudySpark observes repeated actions inside the app, such as notes, flashcards, quizzes, files, and study plans. It does not decide based on one action. You can review why each insight appears, correct it, reset it, or turn personalization off.

13

How Learning Speed Is Measured

StudySpark tracks completed topic activities. A topic needs repeated strong results before it is mastered. It records quizzes, questions, active study time, and days since first practice. Topics that take longer receive more support, and learning speed helps personalize future study plans.

14

How Topic Performance Works

Every quiz question is connected to a topic. StudySpark tracks correct and incorrect answers by topic, and a status appears after enough questions are completed. Weak topics receive more practice recommendations, while strong topics still receive short review sessions. Scores improve as you complete more quizzes.

15

How Memory Tracking Works

StudySpark tracks completed topic reviews. Strong results increase the time before the next review, while lower scores shorten the review interval. Review recommendations are estimates, not guarantees. Regular spaced reviews help students remember information longer.

16

How StudySpark Discovers Effective Study Methods

StudySpark records which study tool was used before a quiz, then compares later performance by method. It waits for enough linked results before making a recommendation. Recommendations may differ by course or topic, and they are estimates that can change over time.

17

How Exam Readiness Works

StudySpark combines quiz performance, study progress, topic strength, and review history to estimate readiness for saved study plans. Readiness is an estimate, not a guaranteed result. Missing topic data lowers confidence, and the system highlights topics likely to have the greatest impact. Readiness updates as you study.

18

How Your Learning Profile Changes

The profile starts with limited information and updates as StudySpark gathers more evidence. Recent study behavior matters more than old behavior, and insights may change as habits and performance change. You can review, correct, manually set, or turn off automatic updates. Old profile changes remain available in the profile timeline.

19

How to Create a Study Match Profile

Open Study Match from the Collaborate section in the sidebar. Choose one saved subject, enter the full course name, exam name, exam date, strong topics, topics where you need help, availability, study methods, group size, meeting preference, and a general location only when meeting in person. Click Save Study Match Profile to store it in this browser for the current user or Guest Mode.

20

About Sample Classmate Profiles

The Study Match prototype uses fictional classmates to demonstrate how matching could work. These profiles are not real students, and no real contact information is stored. A future version would require authenticated accounts, a secure shared database, privacy controls, and clear user consent.

21

How Basic Study Matching Works

StudySpark first checks whether students are studying the same subject. It then compares their course and exam, and exam dates must be close enough for studying together to be useful. Students studying unrelated subjects are not matched. More detailed preferences, such as topics, study times, methods, group size, and meeting style, are considered after basic eligibility.

22

How Topic Strength Matching Works

StudySpark compares topics each student understands well with topics the other student wants to practise. Matches are stronger when both students can help each other, and each complementary topic increases the match score. Students are not judged as good or bad at a subject, and topic information can be changed at any time.

23

Why StudySpark Requires Multiple Patterns

Workload warnings require multiple supported study patterns. One unusual activity does not create a warning. One low quiz, one late-night session, one skipped task, or one long session is treated as an individual fact, not proof of overload. At least two separate supported categories are required for Mild Overload, and at least three separate supported categories are required for High Workload. Strong Overload Pattern also requires enough points and moderate indicators. Indicators using the same exact evidence do not count as separate patterns, and outdated or incomplete evidence does not count. Point totals may be capped when too few separate patterns exist, and the point breakdown explains any safety cap. Lower scores, skipped tasks and long sessions together may support a workload warning when each pattern has enough valid evidence. Workload warnings are not medical or mental-health diagnoses.
